## Five-Category Stakeholder Cascade

Implementation Dependencies (Decision flow downward, Feedback flow upward):

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Senior Leaders → HR → Lower Managers → Employees                            │
│  (Approve)  (Design) (Implement)  (Participate)                              │
│                                                                              │
│                    ← Consultants (Enable/Support) →                          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

This visualization shows that engagement initiatives require multi-level
coordination. Leadership approval is insufficient without manager execution; employee
participation is impossible without manager coaching.

Primary Stakeholders

1. SENIOR LEADERS (CEO, VP People)
   • Role: Approve the program and budgets
   • Interest: Clear ROI and lower turnover costs (save $675K year one if turnover drops)
   • Concern: "Improve engagement WITHOUT budget increases" (cost lowering priority)
   • Engagement: Quarterly business case updates with financial metrics

2. MANAGERS / TEAM LEADS (Implementation Phase)
   • Role: Run recognition, deliver training, coach their teams
   • Interest: Effective tools that don't create excessive workload
   • Concern: Implement non-monetary interventions while employees expect raises
   • Target: Manager confidence rising from 4.2 to 7.5 out of 10
   • Success Factor: Their buy-in is ESSENTIAL—without manager engagement, program will fail

Secondary Stakeholders

3. EMPLOYEES (Individual Contributors)
   • Role: Participate in programs and provide feedback
   • Interest: Frequent, fair recognition and growth opportunities
   • Concern: "45% exceed expectations—where's compensation recognition?"
   • Metric: High participation (≥65%) drives that engagement

4. HUMAN RESOURCES / PEOPLE OPS
   • Role: Design programs, run surveys, track turnover and ROI
   • Interest: Sustainable programs that improve organizational health
   • Support Provided: Templates, vendor coordination, data analytics

5. VENDORS (Training Providers)
   • Role: Deliver recognition tools and training workshops
   • Interest: Successful client outcomes for references
   • Requirement: Must enable manager success (not create extra burden)

Key evidence points
- Rotenstien ,JAMA 2018 ("Prevalence of Burnout Among Physicians"): review of 182 studies (109,628 physicians) reporting high and variable
 burnout measures, highlighting workload and recognition as important factors.
- Panagioti , JAMA Intern Med 2017 ("Controlled Interventions to Reduce Burnout in Physicians"): meta-analysis of 20 comparisons (1,550)
 showing small overall reductions in burnout; organization-directed interventions showed larger effects than individual-only approaches.
- JMIR mHealth uHealth 2021 (analysis of 2019 HINTS wearable data): in a sample of wearable users (1,300), 82% said they would share
 wearable data with their providers, indicating willingness to share through trusted channels.

## Four Key Manager Enablers:

1. TRAINING: 4-hour workshop + monthly coaching = capability building

                    Constraint Management
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Competing Stakeholder Interests Under Zero-Salary Constraint:

Interests don't align perfectly—implementation requires operating around these conflicts:

• SENIOR LEADERS: "Improve engagement without budget increases"
  → Cost Reduction priority

• EMPLOYEES: "45% exceed expectations—where's compensation recognition?"
  → Performance-reward expectation

• MANAGERS: "Implement non-monetary interventions while employees expect raises"
  → Middle management squeeze navigating between HR expectations and employee compensation expectations

Solution Strategy:

Create compensation alternatives that satisfy different needs:
• Recognition platform → addresses public acknowledgment needs
• Growth opportunities → provides career advancement
• Autonomy expansion → provides intrinsic motivation
• Manager coaching → demonstrates investment in employees

Risk and Opportunities:
- RISK: Managers could see recognition as extra work or another box to check; avoid this by
  creating meaningful recognition rules and ensuring less than 2 hours/week time investment.
- OPPORTUNITY: HR and managers both save money if turnover falls—use that shared goal
  to build coalition for non-monetary engagement investment.
