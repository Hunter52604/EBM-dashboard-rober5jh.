#### Secondary Outcome KPIs

**KPI 1: Recognition Coverage Rate**
- **Current Baseline:** 23% employees receive recognition at least weekly (10 points below Gallup 33% benchmark)
- **Target:** 65% (+42 points, 95% confidence from HR=0.72 effect size evidence)
- **Timeline:** Measured monthly during pilot (Week 6, 9, 12 pulse surveys), final Week 13 post-pilot
- **Data Source & Method:** Pulse survey question "I receive recognition for my contributions at least weekly" (Yes/No), cross-validated with Google Sheets audit (600+ total entries ÷ 50 employees ÷ 12 weeks = ~1 recognition per employee per week)
- **Decision Rules:**
  - If <40% by Week 6: Investigate manager non-compliance (Google Sheets shows which managers <10 entries), trigger intervention
  - If 40-55% by Week 12: Partial progress, may need 4-8 week extension to reach 65%
  - If ≥65% by Week 12: Full target achieved, validates HR=0.72 evidence applies to this context

**KPI 2: Authenticity Perception Score** (Addresses Biggest Fear: Cynicism)
- **Current Baseline:** Unknown (assume ~50% skeptical at start based on stakeholder analysis showing employee fear of inauthentic recognition)
- **Target:** >70% employees say "Recognition I receive feels genuine, not forced"
- **Timeline:** Measured monthly (Week 6, 9, 12 pulse surveys) to enable rapid adjustment if cynicism detected
- **Data Source & Method:** Pulse survey question "Recognition I receive feels genuine, not forced" (1-5 scale: 1=Strongly Disagree, 5=Strongly Agree), calculate % saying 4 or 5 (Agree/Strongly Agree)
- **Decision Rules:**
  - If <50% at any checkpoint (Week 6, 9, or 12): EMERGENCY - trigger town hall within 3 business days, collect feedback, adjust approach by following week
  - If 50-70%: Moderate authenticity, continue monitoring and coaching managers on individualization techniques
  - If >70%: Cynicism concern addressed, recognition perceived as genuine

**KPI 3: Autonomy Perception Score**
- **Current Baseline:** 58% ("My opinions seem to count at work" - FEVS-matched question, lowest organizational score)
- **Target:** 66% (+8 points, 88% confidence from β=0.33 autonomy driver evidence)
- **Timeline:** 12-week pilot, measured Week 13 post-pilot survey
- **Data Source & Method:** Pre/post survey, same question as baseline to enable direct comparison
- **Decision Rules:**
  - If <62% post-pilot: Development conversations ineffective or insufficient autonomy opportunities exist in roles, need different approach (more decision-making authority, project choice, process improvement ownership)
  - If 62-65%: Directional improvement (+4-7 points), may reach +8 target with 6-month extension
  - If ≥66%: Full target achieved, development conversations successfully addressed autonomy gap

**KPI 4: Development Perception Score**
- **Current Baseline:** 64% ("I have opportunities to learn and develop professionally" - FEVS-matched, second-lowest organizational score)
- **Target:** 72% (+8 points, 88% confidence from β=0.29 growth driver evidence)
- **Timeline:** 12-week pilot, measured Week 13 post-pilot survey
- **Data Source & Method:** Pre/post survey, same question as baseline
- **Decision Rules:** Same as KPI 3 autonomy (if <68%, investigate development conversation quality; if 68-71%, directional progress; if ≥72%, full target achieved)

**KPI 5: Intent to Leave** (Leading Indicator for Turnover, NOT Actual Turnover in 12 Weeks)
- **Current Baseline:** Unknown % express intent to leave (current 18% turnover = 5-year high, suggests elevated intent)
- **Target:** 10-15% reduction in employees expressing intent to leave in next 12 months
- **Timeline:** 12-week pilot, measured Week 13 post-pilot survey
- **Data Source & Method:** Pre/post survey question "I am seriously considering leaving this organization in the next 12 months" (Yes/No/Unsure), calculate % saying Yes, compare to baseline
- **Decision Rules:** Intent to leave is LEADING indicator, actual turnover is LAGGING indicator. Evidence shows 18-24 months for turnover effects, so 12-week pilot CANNOT measure 18% → 13% turnover reduction yet. If intent to leave decreases (e.g., from 30% → 20%), this predicts future turnover reduction will occur. If intent unchanged, program may not prevent turnover even if engagement increased (employees more engaged but still plan to leave for external reasons like better pay elsewhere).

### Process KPIs (How Implementation Went - Did We Execute the Program Faithfully?)

#### Implementation Quality KPIs

**KPI 6: Manager Training Completion Rate**
- **Target:** 100% of 5 managers complete 2-hour training by Week 2 with >80% post-training confidence in individualized recognition techniques
- **Data Source:** HR attendance records + post-training survey (Likert scale 1-5: "I feel confident giving specific, individualized recognition to my team")
- **Collection Method:** HR tracks attendance in spreadsheet, sends post-training survey immediately after each training session (5 min survey, required before leaving)
- **Measurement Frequency:** Once at end of Week 2 (all 5 trainings completed), audit confirms 5 of 5 attended
- **Decision Rules:** If <100%, program launch delayed until all managers trained (mandatory makeup session within 3 business days if miss initial training)

**KPI 7: Manager Implementation Compliance Rate**
- **Target:** 100% of managers log >40 recognition entries in Google Sheets over 12 weeks (demonstrates sustained weekly implementation: 40 entries ÷ 10 employees per manager ÷ 12 weeks = ~33% of employees recognized weekly per manager, aggregated to 65% organizational coverage when all managers implement)
- **Data Source:** Google Sheets recognition tracker (tab for each manager showing Date, Employee, Recognition Type, Description)
- **Collection Method:** HR audits Google Sheets every Monday morning, counts entries by manager, flags any manager falling behind target trajectory (Week 4 should have ~13 entries, Week 8 should have ~27, Week 12 should have ~40)
- **Measurement Frequency:** Weekly monitoring (every Monday), formal checkpoints Week 4, 8, 12 trigger interventions if needed
- **Decision Rules:**
  - Week 4: If any manager <10 entries, HR schedules 1-on-1 coaching within 2 business days ("What's the barrier? Time? Uncertainty? Team resistance?")
  - Week 8: If manager still <20 entries despite Week 4 coaching, CEO escalation meeting within 3 business days ("Weekly recognition is performance expectation, what support do you need?")
  - Week 12: If 2+ of 5 managers <30 entries (40%+ non-compliance), program cannot succeed with insufficient sample, recommend Continue with compliant managers only or Pivot to different approach

**KPI 8: Development Conversation Completion Rate**
- **Target:** 100% of 50 employees have 1-hour development conversation by Week 8 (autonomy and growth intervention delivered as designed)
- **Data Source:** Google Sheets tracking tab (Manager logs: Employee Name, Conversation Date, Key Themes: Projects interested in, Skills want to build, Autonomy opportunities identified)
- **Collection Method:** Managers log after each conversation (30-second entry), HR audits weekly to track progress toward 100% completion
- **Measurement Frequency:** Weekly progress tracking, final audit Week 8 (deadline for all 50 conversations)
- **Decision Rules:** If <80% by Week 8, offer extension to Week 10 OR allow 30-min streamlined conversations (focus on top priority development area only) to reduce manager time barrier

**KPI 9: Survey Response Rates** (Data Quality Check)
- **Target:** >80% response rate for baseline and post-pilot surveys (40+ of 50 employees), >60% for pulse surveys (30+ of 50)
- **Data Source:** Google Forms response count / 50 employees
- **Collection Method:** HR checks response count daily during 72-hour survey window, sends reminder emails at 24-hour and 48-hour marks to non-responders
- **Measurement Frequency:** Each survey event (baseline Week -3, pulse Week 6/9/12, post-pilot Week 13)
- **Decision Rules:** If <70% response rate, data quality concerns (may not be representative of full organization), include caveat in reporting "Results based on X% response rate, may not reflect all employee views"

#### Stakeholder Engagement KPIs

**KPI 10: CEO Visible Support** (Executive Sponsorship Quality)
- **Target:** CEO completes all sponsorship activities (attends first 15 min of training, models recognition at all-hands Week 2, reads weekly updates, attends decision meeting Week 14)
- **Data Source:** HR observation and documentation (checklist: training attendance Y/N, all-hands recognition Y/N, weekly update emails sent and read receipts if available, decision meeting attendance Y/N)
- **Collection Method:** HR tracks CEO engagement, flags if any commitment not met
- **Decision Rules:** If CEO disengages (stops reading updates, doesn't attend meetings), program credibility at risk - HR escalates "Your visible support is critical for manager and employee buy-in, can we re-confirm your commitment or should we pause program?"

**KPI 11: Manager Satisfaction with Program**
- **Target:** >70% of managers say program is valuable and sustainable workload (not "another HR burden")
- **Data Source:** Week 14 manager satisfaction survey (anonymous 5-question survey: "This program helped me be a better manager" "Workload was sustainable" "I would recommend continuing" "I feel confident in recognition skills now" "I would participate in 6-month extension")
- **Collection Method:** HR sends survey Week 14, collects within 48 hours
- **Decision Rules:** If <70% satisfaction, investigate concerns before recommending continuation (Is time burden too high? Is training insufficient? Are employees not responding positively?)

**KPI 12: Employee Satisfaction with Program**
- **Target:** >65% of employees want program to continue (endorsement for 6-month extension)
- **Data Source:** Week 13 post-pilot survey question "Do you want the recognition program to continue?" (Yes/No/Unsure)
- **Collection Method:** Included in post-pilot engagement survey
- **Decision Rules:** If <65% want continuation despite engagement increasing, investigate disconnect ("Engagement increased but employees don't value recognition - why? Different cause of engagement increase? Recognition feels burdensome to receive?")

#### Resource Utilization KPIs

**KPI 13: Budget Adherence**
- **Target:** Program cost ≤$1,000 (no overrun without CEO approval)
- **Data Source:** Expense tracking spreadsheet (Training $400, Materials $100, Surveys $200, Communications $200, Contingency $200)
- **Collection Method:** HR logs all expenses weekly, shares with CEO in weekly updates
- **Decision Rules:** If projected spend approaching $900, identify cuts before exceeding $1,000 (skip lunch-and-learn, use digital materials only, decline external facilitator if not yet used)

**KPI 14: Time Investment Efficiency**
- **Target:** Manager time ≤1.7 hours/week average (within sustainable workload), HR time ≤4 hours/week average (within HR capacity)
- **Data Source:** Self-reported time logs (optional weekly check-in survey to managers: "Approximately how many minutes did you spend on recognition activities this week?"), HR tracks own time in project management spreadsheet
- **Collection Method:** Manager retrospective discussions Week 4, 8, 12 ask "Is time commitment sustainable?" HR reviews own time weekly
- **Decision Rules:** If managers report >2.5 hours/week consistently (50% over target), workload unsustainable - simplify program (reduce development conversation length, eliminate Google Sheets requirement, shift to biweekly recognition)

### Impact KPIs (Broader Organizational Effects Beyond Direct Program Targets)

#### Organizational Performance KPIs

**KPI 15: Client Satisfaction Stability** (Unintended Consequence Check)
- **Current Baseline:** Client NPS or CSAT score (organizational average, source: CRM or client surveys)
- **Target:** Client satisfaction remains stable or improves (no decline >5 points suggesting program distracted from client work)
- **Timeline:** Measured at Week 14 (compare Q1 2026 client scores to Q4 2025 baseline)
- **Data Source & Method:** CRM data or quarterly client survey results
- **Decision Rules:** If client satisfaction declines significantly (>5 points), investigate whether manager time on recognition diverted from client management or team focus on program distracted from deliverables

**KPI 16: Actual Turnover Rate** (Long-term Impact, NOT Measured in 12-Week Pilot)
- **Current Baseline:** 18% annual turnover (9 of 50 employees per year, 5-year high)
- **Target:** 13% annual turnover (-5 points, 75% confidence from HR=0.72 evidence, BUT requires 18-24 months per studies)
- **Timeline:** 6-month follow-up (June 2026), 12-month follow-up (December 2026), 18-month follow-up (June 2027)
- **Data Source & Method:** HRIS departure data, calculate annual turnover rate = (departures / average headcount) × 100
- **Decision Rules:** 12-week pilot CANNOT measure this (evidence shows 18-24 months needed). If CEO demands turnover metric at Week 14, report baseline 18% and current rate but emphasize "Not statistically significant in 12 weeks, need 6-12 month follow-up." Intent to leave (KPI 5) is leading indicator for this lagging indicator.

#### Cultural/Climate KPIs

**KPI 17: Recognition Culture Strength** (Spillover Effect)
- **Target:** Spontaneous employee-to-employee recognition emerges (employees recognize peers without manager prompting), indicating cultural shift beyond program mechanics
- **Data Source:** Google Sheets shows recognition entries from non-manager employees (optional "Recognize a Peer" tab), open-ended survey feedback mentions peer recognition
- **Collection Method:** Monitor Google Sheets for peer entries, code open-ended survey responses for themes like "team members recognize each other" "culture of appreciation"
- **Decision Rules:** If peer recognition emerges (5+ employee entries in Google Sheets or mentioned by 10+ employees in surveys), strong cultural effect beyond individual manager behavior. If no peer recognition, program limited to manager-employee dynamic only (less cultural transformation).

## Data Collection Plan

### Quantitative Data Collection

#### Organizational Data
**Data Type 1: Turnover Data**
- **Source:** HRIS system (departures by month/quarter)
- **Collection Frequency:** Monthly (pull data first Monday of each month)
- **Collection Method:** HRIS report exported to spreadsheet, calculate monthly turnover rate (departures / 50 employees × 100), annualize for comparison to 18% baseline
- **Analysis Plan:** Track trend over time (Month 1-3 during pilot, Month 4-6 post-pilot, Month 7-12 longer-term). Note: 12-week pilot will NOT show turnover reduction (evidence shows 18-24 months), but tracking establishes baseline and detects any unexpected spikes. Calculate annualized rate: if 2 departures in Q1 2026 = 8 annualized (16% rate, slight improvement from 18% but not statistically significant with N=2).

**Data Type 2: Recognition Frequency Data**
- **Source:** Google Sheets recognition tracker (entries logged by managers: Date, Manager, Employee, Recognition Type, Description)
- **Collection Frequency:** Continuous real-time logging by managers, HR audits weekly (every Monday)
- **Collection Method:** Automated entry count by manager (pivot table in Google Sheets), by employee (how many times each of 50 employees recognized), by week (trend over 12 weeks)
- **Analysis Plan:**
  - Manager compliance: Count entries per manager (target >40 by Week 12), identify non-compliant managers for intervention
  - Employee coverage: Count unique employees recognized each week, calculate % of 50 (target 65% by Week 12)
  - Trend analysis: Plot weekly recognition entries over 12 weeks, look for ramp-up pattern (Week 3-4 low as managers build habit, Week 5-12 stabilized), or decline pattern (Week 3-6 high enthusiasm, Week 7-12 dropping off as managers lose motivation)
  - Public vs Private ratio: Count Recognition Type (Public/Private), if >80% public may indicate managers uncomfortable with 1-on-1 private recognition, if >80% private may indicate employees uncomfortable with public recognition

**Data Type 3: Client Satisfaction Data**
- **Source:** CRM system (NPS or CSAT scores from quarterly client surveys)
- **Collection Frequency:** Quarterly (Q4 2025 baseline, Q1 2026 during pilot)
- **Collection Method:** CRM report showing organizational average NPS/CSAT, compare Q1 2026 to Q4 2025 baseline
- **Analysis Plan:** Simple difference score (if Q4 2025 = 75 NPS, Q1 2026 = 73 NPS, decline of -2 points = acceptable variation, <5 points). If >5 point decline, investigate whether program caused (manager time diverted from clients, team distracted, service quality dropped). If improvement, positive unintended consequence (engaged team serves clients better).

#### Survey Data
**Survey 1: Baseline Engagement Survey (Week -3)**
- **Target Population:** All 50 employees (managers and individual contributors)
- **Sample Size Goal:** >80% response rate (40+ employees) for representative data
- **Timing:** Week -3 (4 weeks before pilot launch), 72-hour response window (Monday-Wednesday)
- **Key Questions:**
  1. Overall engagement index (10 questions: "I feel valued at work," "My contributions are recognized," "I am satisfied with my job," "I would recommend this workplace," etc.) - % favorable, target baseline 62%
  2. Recognition frequency: "I receive recognition for my contributions at least weekly" (Yes/No) - % Yes, expect 23% baseline
  3. Autonomy: "My opinions seem to count at work" (1-5 scale) - % favorable (4-5), expect 58% baseline
  4. Development: "I have opportunities to learn and develop professionally" (1-5 scale) - % favorable, expect 64% baseline
  5. Intent to leave: "I am seriously considering leaving this organization in the next 12 months" (Yes/No/Unsure) - % Yes, baseline unknown
  6. Recognition preferences: "How do you prefer to receive recognition? A) Public in team meetings, B) Private in 1-on-1s, C) Both, D) Prefer not to be recognized" - informs opt-in/opt-out approach
  7. Open-ended: "What would increase your engagement at work?" - captures root causes beyond recognition
- **Administration Method:** Email link to anonymous Google Forms, two reminder emails (24-hour and 48-hour marks), emphasize "Your honest feedback shapes the program"

**Survey 2: Monthly Pulse Surveys (Week 6, 9, 12)**
- **Target Population:** All 50 employees
- **Sample Size Goal:** >60% response rate (30+ employees, lower than baseline/post-pilot due to more frequent surveys)
- **Timing:** Week 6, 9, 12 (monthly intervals to track interim progress and enable rapid adjustment)
- **Key Questions:**
  1. Recognition frequency: "I receive recognition at least weekly" (Yes/No) - track progress toward 65% target
  2. Authenticity: "Recognition I receive feels genuine, not forced" (1-5 scale) - % favorable (4-5), target >70%, if <50% triggers emergency adjustment
  3. Engagement snapshot: 3-question short index ("I feel valued" "I am satisfied" "I would recommend") - track engagement trend before full post-pilot survey
  4. Open-ended: "What would make recognition feel more genuine?" or "What's working/not working with the program?" - qualitative adjustment data
- **Administration Method:** Same as baseline (email link, anonymous Google Forms, 72-hour window, two reminders)

**Survey 3: Post-Pilot Evaluation Survey (Week 13)**
- **Target Population:** All 50 employees
- **Sample Size Goal:** >80% response rate (40+ employees, same as baseline for valid comparison)
- **Timing:** Week 13 (final week of 12-week pilot, before Week 14 CEO decision meeting)
- **Key Questions:** SAME 10-question engagement index as baseline to enable direct before/after comparison, PLUS additional questions:
  - Program continuation: "Do you want the recognition program to continue?" (Yes/No/Unsure) - measures employee buy-in
  - Program impact: "The recognition program has made me feel more valued at work" (1-5 scale Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree)
  - Open-ended: "What worked well about the recognition program?" "What should be changed?" "What else would increase your engagement?"
- **Administration Method:** Same as baseline

**Survey 4: Manager Post-Pilot Satisfaction Survey (Week 14)**
- **Target Population:** 5 managers only (separate from employee survey)
- **Sample Size Goal:** 100% (5 of 5 managers)
- **Timing:** Week 14 after pilot ends, before decision meeting
- **Key Questions:**
  1. "This program helped me be a better manager" (1-5 scale)
  2. "The time commitment was sustainable for my workload" (1-5 scale)
  3. "I feel confident in my recognition skills now" (1-5 scale)
  4. "I would recommend continuing this program" (Yes/No)
  5. "I would participate in a 6-month extension" (Yes/No)
  6. Open-ended: "What barriers did you face?" "What support would help?" "What would you change?"
- **Administration Method:** Anonymous Google Forms (anonymity encourages honest feedback even though only 5 respondents), HR reviews individually to identify themes

### Qualitative Data Collection

#### Interview Data
**Interview Type 1: Manager Coaching Sessions (As-Needed, Weeks 4-12)**
- **Target Participants:** Any manager struggling with implementation (non-compliant with <10 entries by Week 4, or <20 by Week 8)
- **Number of Interviews:** Variable (0-5 depending on how many managers need coaching, estimate 2-3 managers will need 1-2 sessions each = ~4-6 coaching sessions total)
- **Timing:** Triggered by Google Sheets audit showing non-compliance, scheduled within 2-3 business days of detection
- **Key Topics:**
  - "What's preventing you from logging 3-4 recognitions per week?" (time, uncertainty, team resistance, discomfort, workload, don't see value)
  - "When you do recognize employees, what happens? Positive response, awkwardness, deflection?"
  - "What specific support would help you implement more consistently?" (more templates, shorter development conversations to free time, CEO reinforcement, peer examples)
  - "Do you believe this program will work? What makes you skeptical or confident?"
- **Interview Method:** In-person or video, 30-60 min, documented in HR coaching notes (themes tracked for reporting to CEO), used to tailor intervention (if barrier is time, adjust expectations; if barrier is skill, provide additional training; if barrier is belief, provide evidence reminders)

**Interview Type 2: Employee Focus Groups (Optional, Week 6 or 9 if Cynicism Detected)**
- **Target Participants:** 5-7 volunteer employees representing different teams/roles (mix of those receiving recognition and those not, different tenure levels)
- **Number of Interviews:** 1-2 focus groups (1 if <50% cynicism Week 6, 2 if cynicism persists Week 9)
- **Timing:** Week 6 or 9, triggered if pulse survey shows <50% say recognition genuine
- **Key Topics:**
  - "When your manager recognizes you, what goes through your mind? Genuine appreciation, obligation, embarrassment, skepticism?"
  - "What makes recognition feel authentic vs fake in our culture?"
  - "How could managers recognize you in a way that would feel more genuine?"
  - "Is this program addressing what matters to you for engagement, or is recognition missing the point?"
- **Interview Method:** In-person group discussion (45-60 min), CEO or HR facilitates (CEO may be better if employees don't trust HR), notes documented, themes inform rapid adjustment plan

#### Focus Group Data
**Focus Group: Pre-Launch Employee Input (Optional, Week -2)**
- **Participants:** 5-7 volunteer employees (optional activity, only if time permits and want to test messaging)
- **Timing:** Week -2 during preparation phase
- **Key Questions:**

## Data Analysis Plan

### Quantitative Analysis

#### Outcome Analysis - Primary Outcome (Engagement Score Change)
**Statistical Approach:** Simple difference score (no inferential statistics due to lack of control group, before-after comparison only)
- **Calculation:** Post-pilot engagement % (Week 13) MINUS baseline engagement % (Week -3) = change score
  - Example: 70% - 62% = +8 points (full target achieved)
  - Example: 67% - 62% = +5 points (partial success, Continue decision)
  - Example: 63% - 62% = +1 point (minimal impact, Adjust or Pivot decision)

**Comparison Strategy:** Before vs after (Week -3 baseline vs Week 13 post-pilot)
- **No control group:** Cannot compare to "no treatment" condition, so cannot definitively prove causation (10 Barriers Interpretation Bias = HIGH)
- **Mitigation:** Use qualitative data triangulation (if employees cite "manager recognition" in open-ended feedback as reason for engagement increase, strengthens causal inference even without experimental design)

**Significance Criteria:** What counts as meaningful change?
- **Statistical significance not calculated** (N=50 employees too small, no control group, not randomized trial)
- **Practical significance thresholds:**
  - **+8 points (62% → 70%):** Full target, strong practical significance (reaches FEVS top quartile range)
  - **+5-7 points (62% → 67-69%):** Moderate practical significance (directional improvement, Continue decision justified)
  - **+2-4 points (62% → 64-66%):** Small practical significance (some effect but below target, Adjust decision)
  - **0-1 points (62% → 62-63%):** No practical significance (program didn't work in this context, Pivot decision)
  - **Negative change (62% → <62%):** Negative effect (program backfired, Stop immediately)

**Context Considerations:**
- 8-point improvement in 12 weeks is ambitious (most engagement programs show 2-4 points in first year)
- Evidence gave 95% confidence to recognition working generally, but 50% confidence to 12-week timeline specifically
- If result is +4-5 points (half of target), could still be "working as predicted but needs more time" rather than "didn't work"

#### Secondary Outcome Analysis
**Recognition Coverage:** Pulse survey Week 12 "I receive weekly recognition" % MINUS baseline 23% = change score (target +42 points to reach 65%)
- Cross-validate with Google Sheets audit: 600+ total entries ÷ 50 employees ÷ 12 weeks = ~1 recognition per employee per week (aligns with 65% coverage)
- If pulse survey shows 65% BUT Google Sheets shows only 300 entries (half expected), suggests employees perceive more recognition than managers logged (possible informal recognition not logged, or employees overestimating frequency)

**Autonomy/Development:** Same simple difference score approach (post-pilot % minus baseline %, target +8 points each)

**Intent to Leave:** % saying "Yes" to "considering leaving in next 12 months" - compare baseline to post-pilot (expect decrease, e.g., 30% → 20% = 10-point reduction)

#### Trend Analysis Over Time
**Google Sheets Weekly Entries:** Plot recognition entries by week (Week 3-14), look for patterns:
- **Positive trend:** Ramp-up pattern (Week 3-4 lower as managers build habit, Week 5-14 stabilized or increasing) = sustainable implementation
- **Negative trend:** Decline pattern (Week 3-6 high enthusiasm, Week 7-14 dropping off) = unsustainable, managers losing motivation
- **Volatile trend:** Spikes and valleys (some weeks 80 entries, other weeks 20 entries) = inconsistent implementation, may be tied to workload cycles or manager reminders

- **Improving trend:** Week 6 = 50% recognition, Week 9 = 58%, Week 12 = 65% = program gaining momentum, likely to reach target

#### Content Analysis of Google Sheets Recognition Entries
**Quality Coding:** Sample 100-150 entries (25-30 per manager, ~25% of total ~600 entries), code each for:
- **Specificity:** Specific (names exact contribution/date) = 1, Generic (vague "good job") = 0
- **Individualization:** Individualized (tailored to person/situation) = 1, Template (could apply to anyone) = 0
- **Behavior-focus:** Behavior (describes what person did and why it mattered) = 1, Outcome-only (just states result "met deadline") = 0
- **Quality Score:** Sum of 3 codes (0-3 scale, where 3 = high quality specific+individualized+behavior, 0 = low quality generic+template+outcome)

**Calculate % High-Quality Entries:** Count entries with quality score 2-3 (at least 2 of 3 quality elements), divide by total sampled
- If >70% high-quality: Training effective, managers implementing individualized recognition (β=0.41)
- If 50-70% moderate-quality: Mixed implementation, some managers strong, others need coaching
- If <50% low-quality: Managers logging for compliance but not authentic individualization (β=0.19 generic effect, not β=0.41), explains why engagement didn't increase

## Success Criteria and Interpretation

### Success Thresholds

#### Must-Achieve Criteria (Implementation considered successful ONLY if ALL these are met)

**Criterion 1: Manager Implementation Fidelity**
- **Measurement:** Google Sheets entry count per manager (target >40 entries over 12 weeks = avg 3-4 per week)
- **Threshold:** At least 4 of 5 managers (80%) achieve >40 entries by Week 14 (demonstrates sustained implementation)
- **Rationale:** If managers don't implement (log <40 entries), program cannot work regardless of evidence strength. Must have faithful implementation to test whether evidence applies to this context.
- **Decision:** If <80% manager compliance (3 or fewer of 5 managers), program failed due to implementation failure, not evidence failure. Cannot conclude "recognition doesn't work" when managers didn't implement recognition.

**Criterion 2: Recognition Coverage Threshold**
- **Measurement:** Pulse survey Week 12 "I receive recognition at least weekly" (target 65%)
- **Threshold:** At least 50% employees report weekly recognition by Week 12 (up from 23% baseline = +27 points minimum progress)
- **Rationale:** HR=0.72 effect size evidence requires weekly recognition frequency threshold. If only reaching 40% coverage (monthly or less frequent recognition), not hitting evidence threshold and can't expect HR=0.72 effect.
- **Decision:** If <50% coverage despite manager compliance (managers logging entries but employees not perceiving weekly frequency), suggests disconnect between logs and employee experience OR evidence threshold higher than 50% (may need full 65% for HR=0.72 to apply).

**Criterion 3: Authenticity Threshold (Cynicism Addressed)**
- **Measurement:** Pulse survey Week 12 "Recognition feels genuine" (target >70% say 4-5 on 1-5 scale)
- **Threshold:** At least 60% employees say recognition feels genuine by Week 12 (addressing Biggest Fear)
- **Rationale:** If authenticity <60% (more than 40% employees say recognition feels forced/fake), program backfiring per evidence that inauthentic recognition causes negative effects. Even if engagement increases, unsustainable if built on employees perceiving insincerity.
- **Decision:** If <60% authentic despite implementation, program created cynicism risk as feared. Must address before continuing (pivot to employee-designed recognition, add more individualization training, or acknowledge "our culture may not be receptive to recognition approaches").

**Criterion 4: Engagement Directional Improvement**
- **Measurement:** Post-pilot survey Week 13 engagement score (target 70%, baseline 62%)
- **Threshold:** At least 64% engagement by Week 13 (+2 points minimum = directional improvement toward 70% target)
- **Rationale:** 12-week timeline has only 50% confidence per evidence synthesis (18-24 months for full effects). +2 points is small but meaningful progress suggesting program on right track, may reach +8 points with more time. 0-1 point change is not statistically different from baseline (could be measurement error or random fluctuation).
- **Decision:** If <64% (less than +2 points), program not moving engagement despite implementation. Suggests either wrong intervention for this context OR need different timeline/approach.

**Criterion 5: Survey Data Quality**
- **Measurement:** Response rate for baseline (Week -3) and post-pilot (Week 13) surveys
- **Threshold:** At least 70% response rate (35+ of 50 employees) for both surveys
- **Rationale:** If <70% response, data may not be representative (responders could be systematically different from non-responders - e.g., most engaged employees respond, disengaged don't bother). Can't confidently compare baseline to post-pilot if different people responded.
- **Decision:** If <70% response, include prominent caveat in Week 14 report "Results based on X% response rate, may not reflect all employee views. Non-responders may have different engagement levels." Acknowledge data quality limitation when making Continue/Adjust/Pivot decision.

- Google Sheets quality 50-70% high-quality (inconsistent individualization across managers)
- Manager retrospectives report barriers (time pressure, employee discomfort with public recognition, uncertain about development conversation effectiveness)
- Minor negative unintended consequences possible (1-2 employees mention "forced recognition")

**Triangulated Interpretation:** Program showed partial effect but implementation challenges or context gaps prevented full success. Possible causes: (1) Manager non-compliance (some didn't implement faithfully), (2) Weekly frequency threshold not reached (40-54% below HR=0.72 65% threshold), (3) Authenticity concerns (training insufficient or organizational culture resistant), (4) Wrong intervention mix (recognition addressing symptom not root cause like workload), (5) Timeline insufficient (12 weeks too short, need 6 more months for effects to compound).

**Recommendation:** **ADJUST** - Investigate specific barriers through manager interviews and employee focus groups, implement targeted adjustments (if manager non-compliance: add CEO accountability; if authenticity: more private recognition; if workload themes: add workload reduction pilot alongside recognition; if timeline: extend 4-8 weeks with same approach), re-evaluate after adjustment period with clear go/no-go criteria.

#### WEAK SUCCESS or FAILURE Indicators (PIVOT Decision)
**Quantitative Profile:**
- Engagement <64% (+0 to +1 point, no practical significance)
- Recognition <40% (minimal progress, weekly threshold far from reached)
- Authenticity <50% (cynicism materialized, Biggest Fear realized)
- Manager compliance <60% (3+ of 5 managers didn't implement)
- Autonomy/Development negative or flat (interventions ineffective)

**Qualitative Profile:**
- Open-ended themes NOT recognition-related (dominant themes: "burned out," "salary freeze frustrating," "workload crushing," "leadership issues")
- Google Sheets quality <50% high-quality (managers logging for compliance, not authentic individualization)
- Manager retrospectives report employee deflection ("just doing my job"), time unsustainable, value questioned
- Negative unintended consequences (employees mention "forced recognition," "HR corporate theater," manager burnout)

**Triangulated Interpretation:** Program did not work in this context despite implementation efforts. Possible causes: (1) Recognition wrong intervention (root cause is workload/pay/leadership, not lack of recognition), (2) Evidence doesn't apply to 50-person professional services context (gap in evidence base identified in synthesis), (3) Organizational culture incompatible (employees perceive all HR initiatives as insincere), (4) Manager non-compliance reflects lack of belief in program (if managers don't buy in, employees won't either), (5) External factors overwhelming (major client loss, layoffs, economic downturn overshadowing recognition efforts).

**Recommendation:** **PIVOT** - Three legitimate options based on root cause analysis:
1. **Different Intervention:** If qualitative themes identify different root cause (workload, pay, leadership), shift focus to that issue. Maintain recognition as baseline good management practice (costs little to continue) but invest resources in addressing actual driver. Example: "Recognition is necessary but insufficient. 12-week pilot revealed burnout from 60-hour weeks is true driver. Recommend workload reduction pilot (process improvements, staffing increase, client load rebalancing) while maintaining weekly recognition as hygiene factor."

2. **Honest Assessment:** If unclear root cause or multiple factors, gather more data through employee focus groups, exit interviews, stay interviews before trying another intervention. Example: "Engagement unchanged despite faithful implementation (Google Sheets shows 65% coverage, but engagement 62% → 63%). Suggests recognition addressing wrong problem for this organization. Recommend 3-month discovery phase (focus groups: 'What would increase your engagement?') before designing next intervention."

3. **Evidence Doesn't Apply:** If implementation was faithful (100% manager compliance, 65% coverage, >70% quality) but engagement unchanged, acknowledge evidence from 500-5,000 employee orgs may not apply to 50-person firm where personal relationships already strong and recognition less novel. Example: "Program implemented as evidence suggested (weekly individualized recognition β=0.41, 65% coverage HR=0.72) but engagement unchanged (62% → 63%). Possible explanation: In 50-person org, employees already have close relationships with managers, formal recognition adds little to what's already happening informally. Evidence base gap identified in synthesis proved significant. Maintain recognition practice (good management) but don't expect major engagement gains from formalization."

#### Unintended Consequences Assessment

**Positive Unintended Consequences to Watch For:**
1. **Client satisfaction improves:** If Q1 2026 NPS/CSAT increases, engaged team may be serving clients better (spillover effect).
2. **Peer recognition emerges:** If employees start recognizing each other spontaneously (not just managers recognizing employees), cultural shift beyond program design.
3. **Manager skill transfer:** If managers apply individualized recognition techniques to other contexts (performance feedback, coaching, conflict resolution), broader leadership development effect.
4. **Recruitment advantage:** If word spreads about recognition culture, easier to attract talent in competitive market (employer brand benefit).
5. **Cross-team collaboration:** If recognition of cross-functional contributions increases (not just within-team), silos may reduce.

**Negative Unintended Consequences to Watch For:**
1. **Recognition inflation:** If recognition becomes so frequent it loses meaning ("everyone gets participation trophy"), devalues currency of recognition.
2. **Manager burnout:** If 1.7 hours/week recognition time unsustainable on top of existing workload, managers become resentful and quality declines.
3. **Employee discomfort:** If employees uncomfortable receiving recognition (introverts, deflection, cultural norms against self-promotion), creates awkwardness not appreciation.
4. **Favoritism perceptions:** If some employees recognized more than others (intentionally or unintentionally), creates perceived favoritism and damages team dynamics.
5. **Client service decline:** If manager time on recognition diverts from client management, client satisfaction drops >3-5 points.
6. **Superficiality:** If recognition becomes checkbox compliance ("said 3 nice things this week, done"), loses authenticity and becomes "corporate theater" as feared.

**Monitoring Strategy:**
- Pulse surveys ask "Have you experienced any negative effects from the recognition program?" (open-ended)
- Google Sheets distribution analysis (are some employees recognized 20 times, others 0 times? = favoritism)
- Client satisfaction monitoring (NPS/CSAT trends)
- Manager time logs (is 1.7 hours/week sustainable or creeping higher?)
- Open-ended survey themes coding for unintended consequence keywords ("forced," "uncomfortable," "burnout," "favorites," "superficial")

**Decision Protocol if Negative Unintended Consequences Detected:**
- If detected in pulse survey Week 6-9: Adjust immediately (if favoritism, coach managers on equitable distribution; if discomfort, increase private recognition option; if burnout, reduce frequency expectation)
- If detected in post-pilot Week 13: Factor into Continue/Adjust/Pivot decision (if unintended consequences outweigh benefits, Pivot even if engagement increased)
- Include unintended consequences section in Week 14 evaluation report (be transparent with CEO about tradeoffs: "Engagement increased +5 points BUT client satisfaction declined -4 points, need to balance internal focus with external service")
